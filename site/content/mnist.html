I trained the network on 14×14 MNIST digits under two lifts, the pixels themselves ($B = 196$) and random Fourier features of a Gaussian kernel ($D$ = 64, 256, 1024, the tunable parameter count), 114 runs across $\theta$, $N$, the floor and two initial weightings. Then I read $\Sigma_g$ back out of the weights and sampled from it. With minibatch training $\theta$ resolves at most about 12 tiny parts before the network dies, no "fragmented" regime is reached, RFF lifts give two parts (a "1" detector and a "0" detector) in almost every setting, and the readout is accurate on the parts' pixels and essentially blind elsewhere.

The sweep: one part, a few, a dead pool, extinction

Each lift is scaled so the mean feature variance is 1, so $\theta$ is in scaled covariance units (pixels: 94th–99.3rd percentile of off-diagonal $\Sigma_g$; RFF: 64th–95th). The dynamics are the base model with $w_{\min} = \omega/N$.

Key point

Low $\theta$ gives one part on the whole stroke region (a "0"-shaped ring at $\theta = 0.3$), then two or three. Then most neurons lose all inputs before they localise and join the dead pool (every synapse at the floor), the survivors form 7–12 small parts, and at about $\theta = 1.1$ the network is extinct: the count peaks at about 12 (16 in one mean-field twin) and never fragments further. A heavy-tailed init roughly doubles the populated parts and delays extinction, so the transient, not $\theta$ alone, decides which parts are populated.

Why the network dies early, and where the RFF lifts differ

Extinction at $\theta \approx 1.1$ comes although single pixels with variance up to 2.98 would still be stable parts. A neuron starts near the centroid, where its boundary drive $(\Sigma_g h)_b$ is about a row mean of $\Sigma_g$, at most 0.16, far below $\theta$; its weights decay before its embedding can concentrate. With $B$ in the hundreds, a dense iid init puts every embedding within $O(1/\sqrt B)$ of the centroid, so pruning races symmetry breaking, and the dense lateral init initially pulls embeddings further toward the centroid. The collective threshold $\gamma\kappa_0$ (a heuristic crossover; about 0.03 for pixels and 0.0005 for RFF) is far below every $\theta$ used, so lateral binding at $t = 0$ is not what sets the count here.

RFF lifts give two large parts, then the dead pool, then extinction, for every heavy-tailed run at $N = 2048$ and for both inits at $D = 1024$. The exceptions show in the RFF view: the generic init at $N = 512$ mostly gives one major part (at $D = 64$, one throughout), bandwidth ×0.5 gives one at $\theta = 0.1$, and a few runs near extinction leave 3–4 small ones. Mean-field and minibatch agree away from transitions and differ near them (pixels, $N = 2048$, $\theta = 0.6$: 6 live parts and 807 dead in mean-field, 3 and none with minibatches). The floor matters: $\omega = 5$ is extinct from $\theta = 0.6$, $\omega = 0.05$ gives fewer dead neurons and fewer, larger parts.

One network, over training

From $\theta = 0.75$ (pixels) most of the map collapses onto one grey point, the dead pool. Every cluster is a set of clones (within-cluster $L_1$ spread 9.6×10⁻⁶ in the headline run): 2,048 neurons compute at most a dozen distinct functions.

How clusters, the dead pool and the displayed neurons are defined

Clusters: single-linkage components of "$L_1(h_i, h_j) < 0.1$", which does not depend on ordering; in all 114 runs the counts at tolerance 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 agree with each other and with the leader clustering. Live clusters exclude the dead pool; major ones hold at least 1% of $N$. The dead pool is a real cluster: neurons whose every synapse is at the floor, floating at the floor-weighted centroid, with energy far below $\theta$. It is flagged and drawn grey, never counted as a learned part. Displayed neurons: 256 per run in one fixed order, stratified so every cluster has at least $\min(\text{size}, 4)$ members; counts in the sample are not cluster sizes, so the figures quote clusters[].size. Stationarity residual: the mean-field drift $|dw/dt|$ at the snapshot weights, projected onto the floor. Minibatch runs end at their noise floor (headline: mean $1.9\times10^{-4}$, max 0.46 on a few noisy edges); the mean-field twins reach 0.

What the neurons became, as images

Pixel parts are blobs of 2–3 pixels at high-variance stroke locations whose top digits are heavy-ink 8s, 0s and 3s: ink detectors, not digit detectors, and unlike any eigen-image. The two RFF parts are a "1" and a "0" detector; the first eigen-direction contrasts the same two shapes with opposite signs.

The graph, and who gets the neurons

The note guessed neurons in proportion to energy. Here every part's energy is pinned just above $\theta$ (1.04–1.37 in the headline run) while sizes vary 11×: size is inherited from the initial basins and changes with the seed.

Pretend you only have the weights

Every active synapse states one equation, $(HC)_{ib} = \theta + w_{ib}$ or $h_i^\top C h_j = (\theta + w_{ij})/\gamma$. I solve them by masked ridge least squares with the true $\theta$ and $\lambda/\eta$ supplied, and score that one reconstruction three ways: full, scale-invariant and contrast-subspace ($P\hat CP$ against $P\Sigma_gP$). Recovery without knowing $\theta$ was not tested.

  • About as good as knowing the variances. Contrast-subspace cosine 0.17–0.61 over live runs; headline 0.50 against diag $\Sigma_g$ 0.52 and identity 0.35 (the rank-$n_{\text{clusters}}$ truncation of $\Sigma_g$, dead pool counted, a reference point rather than a ceiling: 0.96). For RFF it beats diag: 0.49 against 0.20 at $D = 1024$.
  • BW alone misleads. It ranks the readout below identity and diag in every run (0.66–0.94 against 0.24–0.70): $\hat C$ is nearly zero off the parts, and BW punishes missing variance hardest.
  • Accurate where it measures. On the 30 pixels the parts cover, cosine 0.84; median part-energy error 0.3%.
  • The limit is rank. 67 independent equations for 19,306 unknowns (12 lateral, 55 input); clones multiply equations, not rank.
  • Best mid-training: 0.35 → about 0.58 near step 50, then down as pruning proceeds (last training curve; off-equilibrium readouts).
Other estimators: the note's pseudo-inverse, one edge family, the untrained network

The note's formula $C = H^+RH^{+\top}$ (with $R$ from all lateral weights, clipped ones included, the diagonal filled with the row maximum) and its input analogue $\mathrm{sym}(H^+R_{IB})$ both score below least squares in every live run. The input synapses carry almost everything (least squares on them alone scores about as well as both families), and the readout of the untrained network equals the identity baseline. No hinge penalty for clipped edges and no PSD projection inside the fit; negative eigenvalues are clipped before BW (10–15% of the mass). The parts-sum sketch of the synthetic chapters was not evaluated on these exports.

Moment-based sample synthesis under explicit generative assumptions

Second moments do not determine $p(x)$, so every generator adds a model. The mean is not in the weights; I supply the training mean. Pixels: Gaussian completion $x \sim \mathcal N(\bar x, \hat C)$, clipped to $[0, 1]$. RFF: $g \sim \mathcal N(\mu_g, \hat C)$, then a pre-image $\arg\min_{x \in [0,1]^{196}} \lVert\phi(x) - g\rVert^2$ by projected Adam; exploratory, as a Gaussian $g$ is generally not the image of any digit.

Pixel samples from the weights are the mean digit plus a few flickering blobs (21–35% of the true variance; MMD² 0.027–0.035 against 0.015 for diag $\Sigma_g$ and 0.012 for the true moments). RFF samples are blurry blends of a 0 and a 1, the two learned parts, although real digits survive the pre-image round trip. Blurry blobs are the ceiling of any second-moment generator here, as the true-moment row shows.

What we found

  • amendedDiscrete clusters, their number set by the homeostatic parameters. Yes, but on digits they are tiny blobs or two detectors, at most about 12 with minibatches (16 in one mean-field run), and then extinction rather than finer resolution.
  • failsPopulations mirror eigenmodes, sized by energy, as an expansion code. Parts are local blobs unlike any eigen-image; energies sit just above $\theta$ while sizes vary 11× with the initial basins; members are clones.
  • amendedSolve the weights for $\mathrm{Cov}(g)$. Exact where the weights measure, blind elsewhere: rank-limited by the number of populated parts, so about diag $\Sigma_g$ for pixels and better than it for RFF.
