The first version of this page took the note's Hebb rule literally, a covariance rule on a network of linear resistors, and found that most of what it did was degenerate: one cluster unless something prunes, clusters whose members are exact copies of each other, and an allocation of neurons decided by the starting weights. This version starts again from an energy. Neurons are nodes of a resistor network with an on-site potential that plays the role of a transfer function; each neuron's equilibrium voltage, as a function of the input, is its response function; and the plasticity rule, which strengthens synapses between neurons whose response functions are close, is the gradient of that energy with respect to the weights. The question is whether this model delivers what the note hoped for: diverse neurons within a cluster, a law for how many neurons a mode gets, clusters that relate to the input's eigenmodes, and weights that can be read back for the input statistics.
