Train a network to its fixed point with the covariance rule of Perron clusters, then silence the inputs and let every neuron jitter on its own. Does this spontaneous activity repeat the statistics of the inputs? And do the members of a cluster, which respond identically to every input, also fire together when nothing drives them?

The short answer: the members of a cluster are fully wired to one another, but the wiring is weak next to their input synapses. At rest they are correlated only loosely, with a correlation fixed by how many inputs the cluster listens to. All inputs here are synthetic.

What "spontaneous" means here

A neuron's activity relaxes towards the weighted average of its neighbours. With the inputs on, the network settles at $u = Hg$, and the evoked covariance between neurons is $H\Sigma_g H^\top$. For the spontaneous state we hold the inputs at zero and give every neuron an independent noise current of strength $T$. The activity then fluctuates around zero with covariance

$$C^{\mathrm{sp}} = T\,K^{-1}, \qquad K_{ii} = \sum_j w_{ij} + \sum_b w_{ib}, \quad K_{ij} = -w_{ij}.$$

$K$ is the network's Laplacian: the lateral synapses $w_{ij}$ off the diagonal, each neuron's total conductance on it. Slow, independent, unit-variance noise on the inputs instead would give $HH^\top$, which only depends on the mixtures $h_i$: two neurons with the same mixture would look perfectly correlated whether or not a synapse joins them. Noise inside the neurons tests the wiring itself.

At the fixed point of the rule, a lateral synapse is the evoked covariance of its two ends above the threshold, or the floor $w_{\min}$ if that is larger. Put into (1):

$$T\,\big(C^{\mathrm{sp}}\big)^{-1}_{ij} = -\max\!\Big(w_{\min},\ \frac{\eta}{\lambda}\big(\gamma\,C^{\mathrm{ev}}_{ij} - \theta\big)\Big) \quad (i \ne j), \qquad C^{\mathrm{ev}} = H\Sigma_g H^\top .$$

So the thresholded evoked covariance is not the spontaneous covariance: it is the off-diagonal part of its inverse. In the … trained networks behind this page, the two sides of (2) agree to ….

What survives: who belongs together

Noise spreads only along synapses, and no synapse above the floor joins two clusters. So, up to the floor, the spontaneous covariance falls into one block per cluster. Where two neurons covary by less than the threshold, the evoked activity still shows it (correlation … between clusters in the network above) and the spontaneous activity does not (…).

The same picture answers whether equal mixtures are enough to fire together. They are not. In the network whose threshold lies above every correlation between inputs (show it), … neurons lost every synapse, because no mixture they could reach varies by more than $\theta$. They sit at the same mixture, so their evoked correlation is …; no synapse above the floor joins them, and at rest their correlation is ….

How tightly a cluster fires together

In every trained network here, the members of a cluster are exact copies and each pair of them is joined by a synapse of the same weight $w$. Call $a$ a member's total input conductance. For such a cluster of $n$ neurons, taken on its own (neglecting the floor synapses to the rest of the network), (1) can be inverted by hand:

$$C^{\mathrm{sp}}_{\text{cluster}} = \frac{T}{a + nw}\Big(I + \frac{w}{a}\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top\Big), \qquad \text{correlation } r = \frac{w}{a + w}.$$

The correlation does not depend on the size of the cluster, only on how a lateral synapse compares with the input drive. The fixed point sets that ratio. The cluster's mixture $h$ is a positive eigenvector of its piece of $\Sigma_g - \theta\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$, with eigenvalue $\mu$ (the Perron vector of Perron clusters when that piece has no negative entry); then $a = \tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}\mu$, and with equal learning rates ($\gamma = 1$) $w = a\,|h|^2$. Writing $m = 1/|h|^2$ for the effective number of inputs the cluster listens to,

$$r = \frac{1}{1 + m} \qquad (\gamma = 1).$$

So training does not make a cluster into one unit. Each member keeps its own noise; a pair shares only the fraction $r$ of its variance. Raising the lateral learning rate $\gamma$ strengthens $w$ and, as long as the clusters stay the same, leaves $a$ unchanged; only then do the members lock together.

What is not replayed: how loud

The input synapses that carry the drive to a cluster are the same conductances that hold it down when the inputs are silent. A cluster's mean activity at rest has variance $T/(na)$, up to the floor: it falls with the cluster's size $n$, which the fixed point does not set, and with the input conductance $a$, which grows with the evoked variance above threshold and with the number of inputs the cluster listens to.

So how strongly a cluster responds when driven says little about how much it fluctuates at rest. Over all … clusters the rank correlation between the two variances is …. Among the clusters of one network it is usually negative: below zero in … of the … networks with at least three clusters, with a median of ….

What we found

  • holds Members of a cluster are identical in their mixtures $h_i$ and wired all-to-all with one weight; no synapse above the floor joins different clusters. Neurons that lost every synapse can share a mixture without being wired.
  • fails They do not all fire together at rest. Their correlation is $w/(a+w)$, equal to $1/(1+m)$ for $\gamma = 1$ and a negligible floor: at most one half.
  • amended Spontaneous activity repeats the input statistics only in part. It keeps which neurons belong together, shrinks the correlation between unwired neurons to what the floor carries, and does not carry over the amplitudes: within one network the more strongly driven clusters are usually the quieter ones at rest. The thresholded evoked covariance is the off-diagonal part of its inverse, not its covariance.
The maths: from $\Sigma_g$ to the spontaneous covariance

Noise-driven covariance. With $g = 0$ the energy is $\tfrac12 u^\top K u$ and the dynamics are $\tau\,du = -Ku\,dt + \sqrt{2T\tau}\,dW$. The stationary covariance solves $KC + CK = 2T I$, so $C^{\mathrm{sp}} = TK^{-1}$: the Gibbs distribution $e^{-E/T}$ of the network's own energy.

Relation to the evoked covariance. $H = K^{-1}W_{IB}$, so $C^{\mathrm{ev}} = K^{-1}W_{IB}\Sigma_g W_{IB}^\top K^{-1}$ and

$$W_{IB}\,\Sigma_g\,W_{IB}^\top = T^2\,\big(C^{\mathrm{sp}}\big)^{-1} C^{\mathrm{ev}} \big(C^{\mathrm{sp}}\big)^{-1}.$$

This holds for any weights. At the fixed point, $W_{IB} = \tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}(H\Sigma_g - \theta)_+$ and $W_{II} = \tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}(\gamma C^{\mathrm{ev}} - \theta)_+$ off the diagonal (both with the floor $w_{\min}$), which gives (2) for the off-diagonal entries; the diagonal of $K$ also holds the input conductances.

A cluster of copies (isolated: floor synapses to other neurons neglected, $a > 0$). Let $n$ neurons share the mixture $h$ and write $q = (\Sigma_g h - \theta)_+$, so each has input weights $\tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}q$ and $a = \tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}\mathbf 1^\top q$. Since $K\mathbf 1 = a\mathbf 1$ inside the cluster, $h = q/\mathbf 1^\top q$. On the support $S$ of $q$ this reads $(\Sigma_{SS} - \theta\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top)h = \mu h$ with $\mu = \mathbf 1^\top q$: a positive eigenvector of the thresholded piece (its Perron vector when the piece has no negative entry). Then $v = h^\top\Sigma_g h = \mu|h|^2 + \theta = \mu/m + \theta$ and $w = \tfrac{\eta}{\lambda}(\gamma v - \theta)$, so

$$\frac{w}{a} = \frac{\gamma\mu/m + (\gamma - 1)\theta}{\mu}, \qquad r = \frac{w}{a + w}.$$

$K = (a + nw)I - w\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ on the cluster; inverting gives (3). A member's variance is $T(a + w)/\big(a(a + nw)\big)$, the covariance of two members $Tw/\big(a(a + nw)\big)$, and the variance of the cluster mean $T/(na)$.

All together ($\gamma = 1$, floor neglected): the spontaneous covariance is one block per cluster $c$,

$$C^{\mathrm{sp}}_c = \frac{T\lambda}{\eta}\,\frac{1}{\mu_c\,(1 + n_c/m_c)}\Big(I + \frac{1}{m_c}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top\Big),$$

where $(\mu_c, h_c)$ is the cluster's eigenpair of the piece of $\Sigma_g - \theta\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ the cluster sits on, $m_c = 1/|h_c|^2$, and $n_c$ is its number of neurons, which the fixed point does not determine (see More on the covariance rule).
