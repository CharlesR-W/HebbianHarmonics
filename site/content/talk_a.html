Neurons that fire together wire together

Hebbian learning is purely local: no teacher, no global loss handed down. Yet it works. So what is it computing?

  • It clusters neurons by how they listen to the inputs.
  • You can read the input statistics back out of the weights.

Charles R-W

Why this model? Linear rate neurons with local, correlation-driven weight updates are the standard setting for theories of Hebbian learning. Pehlevan & Chklovskii showed that Hebbian/anti-Hebbian networks of this kind follow from similarity-matching objectives (2015, 2018), and resistor networks with local learning rules are studied, and built, as physical learning machines (Kendall et al. 2020, Stern et al. 2021). The BCM model is a different rule, but its sliding threshold plays the role our regulariser does: it keeps Hebbian growth from running away.

The brain as a resistor network

Neurons are nodes with a voltage, synapses are conductances, and a few input neurons are driven by the rest of the brain.

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{neuron } i:\ u_i \qquad \text{synapse}:\ w_{ij} \\ \text{input } b:\ g_b \end{gathered} \]

  • Symmetric, linear network: the cleanest case.
  • Directed networks: plausibly swap in a directed Laplacian (conjecture).

Squares: input neurons, clamped to voltages that change over time. Circles: interior neurons, settling to the voltage the network sets (warm positive, cool negative). Line width: conductance.

Each neuron is a mixture of the inputs

In the linear network, every neuron's voltage is a fixed blend of the input voltages, set by the weights alone. These blends are the natural variables for interpretability.

\[ u_i = \sum_b H_{ib}\, g_b \]

  • Row \(h_i\) of \(H\) is a probability distribution over the inputs.
  • It is the neuron's harmonic embedding: where it sits between the inputs.

Similar listeners wire together

In these variables, Hebb's rule only asks one thing of a pair of neurons: how alike do they listen to the inputs?

\[ \dot w_{ij} = b - \alpha\, w_{ij} - \tfrac12 D_{ij}^2 \]

\(D_{ij}\): RMS difference between the two neurons' responses. For linear neurons \(D_{ij}^2 = \|h_i - h_j\|^2_{\Sigma_g}\).

  • The covariance rule, \(\dot w_{ij} = \text{reg} + \eta\,\langle h_i, h_j\rangle_{\Sigma_g}\), is its close cousin; the distance version also penalises each neuron's response power.
  • At a fixed point, live synapses satisfy \(\alpha w = b - D^2/2\); pairs with \(D^2 \ge 2b\) disconnect.
