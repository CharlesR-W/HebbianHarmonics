What Hebb does: it groups

Run the covariance rule on 300 neurons and they collapse into a few tight groups in input space, while in the wiring they stay scattered.

shown
  • Each group is a set of neurons with the same embedding: copies of one another.
  • With three independent inputs and \(\theta = 0.4\): one group at each corner.
  • Lower \(\theta\) binds everything into one group; correlated inputs move a group to an edge.

Clusters are Perron parts, not eigenvectors

The note expected one group per eigenvector of the input covariance. Groups sit instead on parts: non-negative Perron vectors of pieces of the thresholded covariance.

derived and shown

\[ A = \Sigma_g - \theta\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top} \]

\[ A_{SS}\,h_S = c\,h_S, \quad h_S > 0 \]

  • \(S\) is the set of inputs a group listens to; \(\theta\) sets how big it is.
  • Precomputed runs, 300–400 neurons, up to 16 inputs (blocks, hierarchy, ring): every group fits its part to cosine \(\ge 0.9999\).
  • With 12–16 correlated inputs the best eigenvector of \(\Sigma_g\) reaches only 0.49–0.87 (with 3 independent inputs \(\Sigma_g = I\): every vector is an eigenvector).
  • Which parts get filled is not fixed by \(\Sigma_g\) and \(\theta\): it moved with the start and the floor (appendix).

A local rule with a global objective

Change the rule slightly and the puzzle dissolves: the network descends its own energy, and each synapse sees exactly its share of the gradient.

derived

\[ \dot w_{ij} = b - \alpha\, w_{ij} - \tfrac12 D_{ij}^2 \]

\(D_{ij}^2\): mean squared difference of the two voltages, \(\lVert h_i - h_j\rVert^2_{\Sigma_g}\). The rule is minus the gradient of the network's mean equilibrium energy plus a weight cost (envelope theorem).

  • A close cousin of the covariance rule: the same Hebbian term, minus each neuron's own power.
  • At a fixed point every live synapse states a distance: \(\alpha w = b - D^2/2\). Pairs with \(D^2 \ge 2b\) disconnect.
