What Hebb does: it groups

Neurons that listen alike fuse into groups; neurons that listen differently disconnect.

\[ \text{link } i\!-\!j \text{ survives} \iff D_{ij}^2 < 2b \]

  • The fusion scale b sets how alike is "alike".
  • Bigger b: fewer, larger groups. Small b: everyone alone.
  • In the real model the middle is messy: many loners, then long chains, then one giant group. Not a few tidy clusters.

Groups are Perron parts, not eigenvectors

Under the covariance rule with a subtractive threshold θ, each group listens to a set of inputs linked by covariances above θ. Raising θ splits these sets along the tree of correlations, coarse to fine.

\[ \begin{gathered} A = \Sigma_g - \theta\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top} \\ A_{SS}\,h_S = c\,h_S,\ \ h_S > 0 \end{gathered} \]

  • One group on its own settles on a Perron part: the all-positive top eigenvector of the block of \(A\) on its inputs \(S\).
  • For unit-variance inputs whose correlations form an exact tree, the parts are the branches above the cut at θ.
  • In large simulations, groups fit their parts far better than any eigenvector of \(\Sigma_g\); which parts fill depends on the start and history.
