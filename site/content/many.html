Does the distance rule sort neurons into a few clusters that the inputs determine? We run it on forty neurons that each mix three of twelve inputs (live, up to twenty-four). In the settings tested they do not: $b$ acts as a resolution, a length that is finer along input directions of high variance, and the state reached depends on the path to it. Tight clusters formed out of a continuum in one setting only, a line of neurons that differ along a single input direction.

Each neuron has fixed attachments to $s$ random inputs, with shares summing to one, and the weights follow the distance rule, with fixed point $w_{ij} = (b - D_{ij}^2/2)_+/\alpha$. A synapse can be live ($w_{ij} > 0$) only between neurons closer than $\sqrt{2b}$, the edge range. A component is a set of neurons connected by live synapses (a neuron with none is a component on its own). A component need not be a cluster: a chain of neighbours can be much wider than $\sqrt{2b}$. So we also report the span of the widest component, the largest distance between two members over $\sqrt{2b}$: above 1 it is a chain, and a component with span at most 1 is a tight cluster.

$b$ sets a resolution, finer along loud directions

A neuron's response is $h_i^\top g$, where $h_i$ is its mixture of the inputs. So for any weights the distance between two neurons is

$$D_{ij}^2 = \Delta h^\top \hat C\, \Delta h, \qquad \Delta h = h_i - h_j,$$

where $\hat C$ is the second moment of the inputs over the training bank. If two mixtures differ by $\delta$ along a unit direction $v_a$ only, $D_{ij}^2 = \hat\lambda_a \delta^2$ with $\hat\lambda_a = v_a^\top \hat C v_a$, the inputs' variance along that direction on the bank: a difference costs more along a loud direction. From $W = 0$ an edge starts to grow exactly when $D_{ij}^2 < 2b$, that is when

$$\delta < \delta_{on} = \sqrt{2b/\hat\lambda_a}.$$

For a pair of neurons on its own, started from $W = 0$, this is also exactly the condition for a live synapse at the fixed point; in a population it is only the condition for the edge to start growing. At a fixed $b$, a pair that differs along a loud direction stays apart even when the difference is small, and a pair that differs along a quiet one fuses even when it is large.

Once an edge grows, the pair moves together and the edge grows further. When $b > \alpha/4$ (unit attachment), a fused pair therefore survives out to a larger separation $\delta_{off}$ than the one at which it formed, by a ratio $\delta_{off}/\delta_{on}$ that does not depend on the direction: at $\alpha = 0.1$ and at $\alpha = 0.01$ (both at $b = 0.1$). At $\alpha = 1$, $b = 0.1$ the condition fails and the ratio is : no hysteresis.

The closed form

For two neurons with unit input conductance, $D^2(w) = D_0^2/(1 + 2w)^2$ with $D_0^2 = \hat\lambda_a\delta^2$, and at fixed $b > \alpha/4$ a fused pair survives up to (for $b \le \alpha/4$, $\delta_{off} = \delta_{on}$)

$$\begin{gathered}\delta_{off} = \sqrt{\frac{32\,(b + \alpha/2)^3}{27\,\alpha^2\,\hat\lambda_a}}, \\ \frac{\delta_{off}}{\delta_{on}} = \sqrt{\frac{16\,(b + \alpha/2)^3}{27\,\alpha^2\, b}}.\end{gathered}$$

Condensation: a line of neurons along one direction

In the default view (the condensation tour) the uncoupled line is connected, so the prediction is one component or two. As the edges grow, members are pulled towards local centres, the links between centres stretch past $\sqrt{2b}$ and die, and the line breaks into a few tight pieces. What is missing

The model has a length scale, $\sqrt{2b}$, but nothing that makes a neuron choose among its inputs. Random attachments place the uncoupled responses on a continuum, and cutting a continuum at a length gives chains and a giant component, not a few clusters. Clean clusters appear when the attachments already separate the neurons, for example when each neuron is wired to one block only (see Who gets the neurons?); the random multi-direction settings tested here did not sort the neurons that way.
