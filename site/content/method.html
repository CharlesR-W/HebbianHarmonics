Appendix. The engineering choices behind every chapter, what was not done, and how to reproduce the numbers. It applies to the whole path from Primer to Nonlinear neurons.

Which conclusions are about the model, and which are about my engineering? This chapter lists every choice that could matter, with the alternative and what the alternative would change in the other chapters. Then it lists what was not done and why each gap matters, and says how to reproduce every number on the page. The values quoted in the tables below are read from the precomputed sweeps' own configuration files, and the checks in the middle run in your browser.

What was not done, and why it matters

  • A stability criterion for structural change. The Hessian of $J$ along a branch of fixed points would locate exactly where a fused state loses stability, a saddle-node at which a component splits or merges. The two-neuron case is done in closed form (fusion), but for populations the hysteresis windows are known only to the resolution of a grid of $b$, and the question of when a component breaks is answered empirically.
  • Lagged statistics and coherent sets. The rule sees only equal-time second moments of the responses, $\mathbb E[(F_i - F_j)^2]$; for linear neurons these are equal-time second moments of the input, and for nonlinear neurons they depend on more of the input distribution, but never on its timing. Inputs with temporal structure, and a rule driven by lagged correlations, would group neurons by what persists over time (the coherent sets of a dynamical system) rather than by what co-varies. That might give clusters a meaning that equal-time statistics cannot. Not tried.
  • A normalised generative objective. The allocation law appeared only under an added principle (similarity matching with a fixed amplitude per neuron) that is not derived from the conductance energy. An objective inside the model that makes the network reproduce its input statistics, with a normalisation per neuron, is the natural candidate for supplying both the redundancy cost and the amplitude regulation. It was not formulated.
  • Real data. Every input is a synthetic Gaussian with a stated covariance, simulated in the browser, as the author decided for this version. A lifted real dataset would test whether the conclusions survive non-Gaussian statistics and spectra that are not planted.
  • Larger sizes. The sweeps stop at $N = $ and $B = $ . The note's picture (many neurons per mode, many modes) and any statement about how cluster statistics scale with $N$ need larger networks.
  • Other potentials and non-Gaussian inputs. Only the two-slope potential was run, and only Gaussian banks. The engine already offers heavy-tailed (Laplace) banks with the same covariance. A saturating transfer, or heavy-tailed inputs, change response functions beyond their second moments, which is exactly where the diversity verdict could move.
  • The allocation ingredients not tried. Non-negative similarity matching with rectified outputs (the most natural specialising principle), a global search for the integer-optimal pure allocation that projected gradient does not find, a soft amplitude penalty, sparsity, noise or competition between neurons, and plastic attachments constrained to fix each neuron's response variance instead of its input conductance (allocation).

Where the specification was wrong

Writing the experiments and chapters corrected a few statements in SPEC.md and in the first draft of the findings. The page follows the corrections.

  • The SPEC's two-neuron example ($b = 0.2$, $\alpha = 0.1$, two stable states) needs an uncoupled distance $D_0^2 = 1$. With unit-variance inputs it is monostable (fusion).
  • A uniform quadratic leak does not remove the blind subspace of the readout, even though it makes $H\mathbf 1 \ne \mathbf 1$. Only a leak that varies across neurons can; it then recovers the full second moment only if the full-space operator reaches full rank (78 for 12 inputs), as it did in the tested networks (readout).
  • With random attachments to several input modes, the middle range of $b$ is singletons, chains and a giant component, not "a few fused clusters". At small $\alpha$ the components are tight, but their number has no stable plateau (fusion). The exception is a line of neurons along a single mode, which at small stiffness condenses into a few tight clusters whose number depends on $b$ and on history (many inputs).
  • The resolution law is exact only for the training bank's second moment, and self-consistent fusion moves it both ways: finer from a cold start, coarser along a downward continuation (many inputs).
  • Some hinge-readout numbers at small $b$ in the readout sweep came from an optimiser that had not converged. The readout chapter minimises the hinge exactly and does not quote them.

How to reproduce

Model and tests. The numpy reference is hebb/onsite.py; the browser engine is site/js/onsite_engine.js, documented in site/js/ONSITE_ENGINE.md. Run every test with

uv run --with pytest pytest -q tests/

tests/test_onsite.py checks the reference: equilibria are stationary and minimal, the envelope identity, the harmonic linear case, and the fixed point with the two-neuron bistability. tests/test_onsite_parity.py drives the browser engine under node (tests/onsite_runner.mjs) and compares it with numpy. Random streams and attachments match bit for bit; equilibria, distances, drift, energy and the harmonic matrix match to $10^{-9}$; 60-step Euler trajectories match to $10^{-9}$ and runs to a fixed point to $10^{-7}$ in $W$; components, cluster statistics, the two-neuron bistability and MDS are also covered. The model arrays are built in Python and passed to node, because the two sides use different generators. node tests/onsite_bench.mjs measures the engine's speed. tests/test_engine_parity.py does the same job for the old rule's engine.

Sweeps. Each precomputed figure comes from one script, hebb/sweeps2/<name>.py, writing site/data/sweeps2/<name>.json; the names are fusion, resolution, diversity, eigen, allocation and readout. Run one with uv run python -m hebb.sweeps2.<name>, or all six with hebb/sweeps2/run_all.sh. They run on CPU in minutes, with no GPU. Each JSON carries its configuration, its seeds and a description of every field, and the chapters read their numbers from those files at load time. The written record is docs/findings_v2.md, whose closing section of corrections overrides the findings above it.

The page. python3 -m http.server 8000 --bind 127.0.0.1 -d site, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000/. There is no build step. Every live figure states its settings in its caption and its state in the URL, so a view can be shared as a link.
