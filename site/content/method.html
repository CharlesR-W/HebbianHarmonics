The note leaves a lot of freedom: what the regulariser is, how small "too small" is, what a cluster is, what counts as recovering a covariance. Every chapter had to fix these somehow. This chapter lists what I chose, what else I could have chosen, and how much it would matter, so you can tell which conclusions are about your idea and which are about my engineering.

Decisions, defaults and alternatives

Defaults throughout unless a chapter says otherwise: $\lambda = \eta = 1$, $\gamma = 1$, $w_{\min} = 10^{-4}$, $W_{IB} \sim \mathrm{Exp}(0.2)$, $W_{II} \sim U(0, 0.03)$, mean-field, run until the residual $\max|dw/dt| < 10^{-5}$.

What recovering $\Sigma_g$ says about $p(\text{stimulus})$

The note argues that for a rich enough lift $g = \phi(x)$ the covariance of $g$ determines $p(x)$. The conclusion is right for characteristic kernels, but for a stronger reason: the mean embedding $\mathbb E\,\phi(x)$ already determines $p$. Second moments are the mean embedding under the product kernel $k^2$, which is again characteristic for a Gaussian $k$. With random Fourier features this becomes concrete: since $\cos a\cos b = \tfrac12[\cos(a-b) + \cos(a+b)]$, the second moments of features at frequencies $\omega_i, \omega_j$ are the characteristic function of $p$ sampled at $\omega_i \pm \omega_j$. Hebb, though, sees the centred covariance, the kernel covariance operator, which subtracts the product of the means.

Three caveats keep "we recovered $\Sigma_g$" from meaning "we recovered $p$". First, with $D$ random features nothing is exactly characteristic; you get finitely many Fourier samples. Second, equilibrium weights store only a parts-sketch of $\Sigma_g$, so even a perfect lift would be read through a rank-$K$ bottleneck. Third, turning second moments into samples needs an explicit generative assumption (for instance, Gaussian in feature space followed by a pre-image step), and the samples inherit that assumption. With the pixel lift (a linear kernel), $\Sigma_g$ is the pixel covariance and determines $p$ only if $p$ is Gaussian.

Where the note and this page part ways

  • The sign. The note's displayed rule has a minus sign in front of $\langle h_i, h_j\rangle_{\Sigma_g}$, which would be anti-Hebbian; the sentence after it ("we cut the connection between two neurons to the extent that their harmonic embeddings misalign") wants a plus, and that is what I use.
  • The regulariser's constant must be negative. With $\rho(w) = w_0 - \lambda w$ and $w_0 \ge 0$, any $\Sigma_g$ without negative entries gives one cluster. Clusters need $w_0 = -\eta\theta < 0$ (or anticorrelated inputs, which is the same thing), and "stops weights getting too small" becomes a separate hard floor.
  • Clusters are parts, not eigenmodes. Non-negative Perron vectors of $\Sigma_g - \theta\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ on a support; eigenvectors only when the top eigenvector happens to be non-negative.
  • The knobs are $\theta$ and $\gamma$, not "modes" and "functions per mode". $\theta$ sets which parts exist, lateral plasticity sets how many are resolved, $\lambda$ only sets the scale, and nothing sets the number of functions per mode.
  • No spectral filter at equilibrium. Any allocation is a fixed point; the filter $n_k \propto \lambda_k^\beta$ comes from the transient, and it is steep, not proportional.
  • No reservoir. With dense wiring, cluster members are exact clones; more neurons give more copies, not more surface area.
  • The readout is a least-squares problem on active edges, not $C = H^+RH^{+\top}$; it recovers only the populated parts, up to scale and a $\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ shift.

Nonlinearity, three ways

"Add a nonlinearity" can mean three different things here, and only one of them keeps the note's geometry.

  1. Rectify the voltages of a circuit with non-negative inputs. This does nothing. If $g \ge 0$ (firing rates), every $u_i = h_i^\top g$ is a convex combination of non-negative numbers, so $\mathrm{relu}(u) = u$ by the maximum principle. A rectifier only bites with a threshold.
  2. Put the nonlinearity inside the circuit (nonlinear conductances or nodes). Then $u$ is no longer $Hg$ for a fixed $H$: each neuron's "embedding" depends on the stimulus, and the whole construction (embeddings as functions of the weights alone, the closed ODE, the readout) is gone. That is a different project.
  3. Keep the circuit linear and rectify the output that plasticity sees. Voltages are still $u = Hg$ and the embeddings are unchanged; the rule sees rates $r_i = \mathrm{relu}(u_i - \text{threshold})$. For Gaussian inputs, $\mathrm{Cov}(r_i, r_j)$ is a dual activation (the arc-cosine kernel) of $h_i^\top\Sigma_g h_j$ and the two variances, so the inner product of embeddings is replaced by a nonlinear function of it. That is the Rectified neurons chapter.

Caveats, and what was not done

  • A tiny residual does not certify stability. An exactly symmetric state can sit on an unstable fixed point with residual near zero (the hierarchical "half" part persisted well past where it is unstable). Private jitter of about $10^{-3}$ per step exposes this; the sweeps that rely on it say so.
  • The dependence of the extinction threshold on $\omega = Nw_{\min}$ is empirical; I have no derivation.
  • The coarse-to-fine gating rule $\theta_c = \gamma\kappa_0$ is a heuristic. It predicts every tested transition on block and hierarchical covariances within 0.03–0.08, but was not tested quantitatively on power-law spectra.
  • Allocation under private per-neuron noise was not measured, only shared minibatch noise.
  • The exact non-equilibrium readout (using the known $dw/dt$) is in the engine but was not evaluated in the sweeps.
  • Stacked layers were deferred. A purely linear stack cannot add anything: $u_2 = H_2u_1 = H_2H_1g$, so a second-layer neuron's embedding over the inputs is a convex combination of first-layer embeddings, and a clustered first layer hands the second a $K$-dimensional input. Layer 2 can only regroup layer-1 parts. New features need a nonlinearity between layers.
  • Everything outside MNIST uses synthetic covariances (blocks, hierarchies, rings, power laws); CIFAR was not tried.
