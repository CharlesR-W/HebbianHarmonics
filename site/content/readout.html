The note's most striking claim was that the weights can be read backwards: compute each neuron's embedding from the weights, then solve for the input statistics. For linear neurons under the distance rule this works, exactly, and this chapter says under which conditions. Each live synapse turns out to be a measurement of one squared distance between two neurons, which is one linear equation on the input second moment; with enough of them, and many more neurons than inputs, the contrast part of that moment is determined.

A change of rule

The covariance rule of the previous chapter does not support this: at its fixed point the weights record only the populated parts (below). This chapter uses the distance rule of the idea chapter,

$$\frac{dw_{ij}}{dt} = b - \alpha w_{ij} - \tfrac12 D_{ij}^2, \qquad D_{ij}^2 = \mathbb E_g\big[(u_i - u_j)^2\big], \qquad w_{ij} \ge 0,$$

which is gradient descent on the network's own equilibrium energy, averaged over inputs, plus a cost $\tfrac\alpha2 w^2 - b w$ per synapse (the envelope theorem: the derivative of the minimised energy in $w_{ij}$ is $\tfrac12(u_i - u_j)^2$ at the minimiser). It is a close cousin of the covariance rule: $-\tfrac12 D_{ij}^2 = \mathbb E[u_i u_j] - \tfrac12\mathbb E[u_i^2] - \tfrac12\mathbb E[u_j^2]$ is the same Hebbian coactivity term minus each neuron's power, and $b$ plays the part of the threshold. Two things change in the setup: only the lateral synapses $w_{ij}$ are plastic, while each neuron's attachments $a_{ib}$ to the inputs are fixed ($s = 3$ random inputs, shares summing to one); and the expectation is over a fixed training bank of $S$ stimuli, so $\hat C = G^\top G/S$, the bank's uncentred second moment, stands where $\Sigma_g$ stood. Neurons are linear throughout this chapter; the next chapter adds on-site potentials.

Each live synapse is a measurement

At a fixed point, every pair of neurons is in one of two states:

$$\begin{aligned} w_{ij} > 0: &\quad D_{ij}^2 = 2\,(b - \alpha w_{ij}) \quad\text{exactly},\\ w_{ij} = 0: &\quad D_{ij}^2 \ge 2b . \end{aligned}$$

Away from the fixed point the stored value is off by exactly twice the drift, $2(b - \alpha w_{ij}) - D_{ij}^2 = 2\,dw_{ij}/dt$, so the plasticity residual bounds the error of every live entry. Reading the table needs $b$ and $\alpha$.If $b$ and $\alpha$ are unknown, the live-edge equations are homogeneous in $(C, \alpha, b)$, so the readout is determined up to one common factor. I checked on the configurations of the live figure that the extended operator then has a one-dimensional kernel and that fixing $b$ returns the true $\alpha$; it is not part of the sweeps.

For linear neurons $u = Hg$, with the harmonic kernel computed from the plastic weights and the fixed attachments alone ($L$ the graph Laplacian of $W$, $A$ the $N \times B$ attachments):

$$H = \big(L + \operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf 1)\big)^{-1} A ,$$

With $d_{ij} = h_i - h_j$ the distance is a quadratic form, $D_{ij}^2 = d_{ij}^\top \hat C\, d_{ij}$, so each live edge is one linear equation in an unknown symmetric matrix $C$, and each dead pair one linear inequality:

$$\begin{aligned} d_{ij}^\top C\, d_{ij} &= 2\,(b - \alpha w_{ij}) && \text{(live)},\\ d_{ij}^\top C\, d_{ij} &\ge 2b && \text{(dead)} . \end{aligned}$$

This is the note's $HCH^\top = R$, with distances in place of covariances. Since $H\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$, every difference is a contrast, $d_{ij}^\top\mathbf 1 = 0$, and

$$\begin{aligned} d^\top \big(C + \mathbf 1 a^\top + a \mathbf 1^\top\big)\, d &= d^\top C\, d + 2\,(d^\top \mathbf 1)(a^\top d) \\ &= d^\top C\, d . \end{aligned}$$

for every $a \in \mathbb R^B$. So the measurements see $C$ only through $P C P$, $P = I - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/B$: the second moment of the input with its instantaneous mean over the $B$ inputs removed. The common mode, $\mathbf 1 a^\top + a\mathbf 1^\top$ ($B$ of the $B(B+1)/2$ dimensions), is invisible in principle: the network cannot tell whether all its inputs tend to rise together. Whether $P\hat C P$ is determined is then a rank question. In an orthonormal contrast basis $Q$ write $c_e = Q^\top d_e$ for edge $e$; the live edges define the measurement operator $\mathcal M: Y \mapsto (c_e^\top Y c_e)_{e\ \text{live}}$ on the $B(B-1)/2$-dimensional space of symmetric contrast matrices (66 for $B = 12$), and $P\hat C P$ is determined if and only if $\mathcal M$ has full rank.

Key point

For linear neurons under the distance rule: exact identifiability of the training-bank contrast second moment $P\hat C P$, for stationary noiseless weights, known attachments and rule parameters ($b$, $\alpha$), and a full-rank, well-conditioned live-edge operator $\mathcal M$ (derived). The common mode is never seen.

The estimator, as implemented on this page and in the sweeps

Masked ridge least squares. $\hat Y = \arg\min_Y \sum_{e\ \text{live}} \big(c_e^\top Y c_e - 2(b - \alpha w_e)\big)^2 + \rho\,\lVert Y\rVert_F^2$ with $\rho = 10^{-10}\sigma_{\max}^2$ ($\sigma_{\max}$ the largest singular value of $\mathcal M$). Along singular directions of $\mathcal M$ with $\sigma_k \gg 10^{-5}\sigma_{\max}$ this is plain least squares; weaker directions are shrunk by the factor $\sigma_k^2/(\sigma_k^2 + \rho)$, and unmeasured ones are set to zero. That zero is a prior, not data, and so is the shrinkage: an operator of full rank but with tiny singular values identifies the target in exact arithmetic without the ridge returning all of it.

Dead-pair hinge (optional). Add $\sum_{\text{dead}} \big(2b - c^\top Y c\big)_+^2$, the squared violation of the inequalities in (3). The page minimises this convex, piecewise-quadratic objective exactly (semismooth Newton with an exact line search, started from the ridge solution). The sweep used L-BFGS from the ridge solution instead; rerunning some of its small-$b$ configurations, I found that it stops with an objective orders of magnitude above the minimum, so the sweep's hinge cosines at small $b$ describe that early-stopped iterate, which mostly scores somewhat higher than the minimiser. At full rank the two agree.

PSD projection of $\hat Y$ (negative eigenvalues set to zero), then back to input coordinates, $Q\hat Y Q^\top$, which is a matrix of the form $PCP$.

Scores. The Frobenius cosine $\langle X, T\rangle_F/(\lVert X\rVert_F \lVert T\rVert_F)$ against $T = P\hat C P$ (what the weights encode) and against $T = P\Sigma_g P$ (the population, which differs by sampling); spectra; the Bures–Wasserstein distance on the contrast space divided by $\sqrt{\operatorname{tr} T}$. The rank of $\mathcal M$ is the number of singular values above $10^{-8}\sigma_{\max}$, computed by a Householder QR and a one-sided Jacobi SVD (the normal equations would square the condition number).

Baselines, all fitted to the same live measurements and scored the same way: a multiple of the identity $cI$ (least squares), a non-negative diagonal covariance (NNLS), the formula applied to an untrained random network with the same number of live edges at random pairs and $w \sim U(0, b/\alpha)$, and to the trained weights shuffled over pairs. $W = 0$ itself has no live edge and gives nothing to read.

The last tour points at what makes the readout work: the number of live edges, which grows faster than $N$ at fixed $b$. The precomputed runs below repeat the readout at $B = 12$ for $N$ from 40 to 500, three seeds and three values of $b$ each (all 45 runs converged, residual at most …).

The network does not need to fuse for this: at $b = 0.01$ the full-rank networks at $N = 320$ and $500$ are still in … and … components. Once the rank is full, more neurons also condition the operator better: at $b = 0.1$ the mean $\log_{10}$ condition number falls from … at $N = 40$ to … at $N = 500$.

At full rank the recovery has cosine at least … with $P\hat C P$, with one exception, which is the reason for "well-conditioned" in the statement above: at $N = $ …, $b = $ …, seed …, the operator has rank 66 at the $10^{-8}$ tolerance but … at $10^{-4}$ (condition number …), and the ridge returns cosine …. Against the population $P\Sigma_g P$ the full-rank cosine is … on average: that gap is sampling, not the readout.

Three errors, kept apart

A readout can be wrong for three unrelated reasons, and the page reports them separately (tiles in the live figure, panel (d) above).

  1. Fixed point. The identity (1) holds only at stationarity; the error is twice the residual drift. In the runs above it is at most ….
  2. Inverse problem. The error of the recovery on the well-determined subspace (singular values above $10^{-4}\sigma_{\max}$), at most … above; plus whatever share of the target lies outside it, which the ridge shrinks or sets to zero. Below full rank that share dominates, and no estimator removes it without a prior.
  3. Sampling. The weights encode $P\hat C P$ for the $S$ inputs they were trained on, not $P\Sigma_g P$; the gap, … here, shrinks only with $S$.

The covariance rule's readout: a sketch of the parts

Under the covariance rule the same idea gives much less. At its clustered fixed point the weights are a function of the parts, their conductances and their sizes, plus inequalities from the synapses at the floor: each active input synapse gives one equation, $(\Sigma_g h_k)_b = \theta + c_k h_{kb}$ on part $k$'s support, the lateral synapses add only the energy of each populated part, and clones multiply the equations without adding rank. For 12 inputs in blocks the measurement operator had rank 5–24 of the 78 entries of $\Sigma_g$. So that readout is accurate on the populated parts and blind elsewhere: a sketch of the parts, not of $\Sigma_g$. More on the covariance rule

What this does not give

Only contrasts: the common mode is invisible without an on-site potential (a leak that varies across neurons can expose it, fragilely). Only the training bank's uncentred second moment, not the population's, and not the input distribution: a second moment does not determine a distribution (a mixture of two stimuli with weights $p$ and $1 - p$ has centred covariance $p(1-p)\,\delta\delta^\top$, $\delta$ the difference of their features, whichever way round the weights go). Only with the attachments $A$ and the rule parameters known. And only for linear neurons: with an on-site potential, $D_{ij}^2$ is no longer a quadratic form in any fixed matrix of input moments, and what the weights state is a partial table of response distances (the next chapter).

Details: why fusion makes the readout fragile

A relative weight error $\varepsilon$ changes the stored distance by $2\alpha w\,\varepsilon$, so the relative error of $D^2$ is $\varepsilon$ times the noise gain

$$\frac{2\alpha w_{ij}}{D_{ij}^2} = \frac{2b - D_{ij}^2}{D_{ij}^2} .$$

It is of order one for edges near the end of their range ($D^2 \approx b$) and diverges as clusters tighten ($D^2 \ll 2b$). In a fused network $w \approx b/\alpha$, and the information sits in the tiny deviations from it. The noise also moves $H$, which the readout rebuilds from the same noisy weights. Just above percolation the readout survives 1% noise; fully fused, fusion amplifies the noise and can badly degrade the recovery (cosine 0.70–0.99 in the fused $N = 400$ runs of dist_B.json; panel (c) of the sweep above, and the noise-gain tile of the live figure).

Details: a quadratic leak, uniform or heterogeneous

Any on-site potential breaks $H\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$. The simplest is a leak, $V_i(u) = \tfrac12 k_i u^2$, which keeps the neurons linear: $H = (L + \operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf 1) + K)^{-1}A$ with $K = \operatorname{diag}(k)$. The blind subspace survives as long as the leak is proportional to each neuron's input conductance:

$$\begin{gathered} \big(L + \operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf 1) + K\big)\mathbf 1 = A\mathbf 1 + k, \\ k = c\,A\mathbf 1 \ \Rightarrow\ H\mathbf 1 = \frac{\mathbf 1}{1 + c} . \end{gathered}$$

Then $d^\top\mathbf 1 = 0$ still, and the same $B$ directions are invisible. Here every neuron has total input conductance 1, so a uniform leak is exactly this case: $H\mathbf 1 \ne \mathbf 1$, yet nothing changes. Only a leak that varies across neurons can make $d^\top \mathbf 1 \ne 0$; then the unknown is the full $\hat C$ (78 entries). Whether it is recovered is the same rank question as before, now on the full space: it needs the live-edge operator to reach rank 78, and to be well conditioned there. A heterogeneous leak does not guarantee that. Four neurons with identical attachment rows $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ and leaks $0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8$ have harmonic rows with different sums, but every difference between them is parallel to $(1, 1)$, so even all six pairs measure only one linear combination of the three entries of a $2\times 2$ $\hat C$. In the networks tested here the rank does reach 78 and $\hat C$ is recovered, an empirical result for these configurations (below). The catch is the same as before: fusion pulls connected neurons together, $d^\top\mathbf 1$ shrinks with them, and the information about the common mode sits in ever smaller numbers.
